# Browser comparison

Captured in real browser at 1440×1000: qa/redesign/concept-a.jpg, concept-b.jpg, concept-c.jpg. Each is a small coded homepage prototype with a working demo add button and in-page navigation. No screenshot was fabricated from code.

| Direction | Expression | Coherence | Originality | Shopping clarity | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Campaign | Monumental geometric wordmark with a photographic object crossing its lower edge | One cold photographic world and cobalt ink; cutouts can carry every SKU | Strongest move away from incumbent; no rectangular hero picture or split scaffold | Product, size, price and quick add visible in the same scene | Selected |
| B — Journal | Full-bleed macro and condensed orange type | Convincing editorial cover | Still depends on the same close bread photograph; the block-over-image treatment is more familiar | Purchase sits after the photographic cover | Retain as unchosen exploration |
| C — Gallery | Lavender stage, italic serif, central loaf | Calmer, lyrical | Changes mood but risks recreating the generic premium serif recipe | Headline intersects loaf; shopping hierarchy weaker | Retain as unchosen exploration |

A wins on expression and operational clarity, not ease of implementation. It requires a complete consistent image series and a responsive composition rebuilt rather than scaled down. Risks to resolve: wordmark width, loaf/copy collision, overly tiny utility text, and turning the lower page back into uniform product boxes.

Final implementation: extend A into a cobalt catalog with open irregular shelves, one oversized featured product, a compact ingredient/process section, full-width photographic detail interlude and transactional controls with clear large text. Remove prototype's decorative ingredient circle. Every product gets the same photographic light and transparent material treatment. Font hierarchy: Unbounded for the brand only; Golos Text for Russian display and interface.

User explicitly delegates choice. Seed assigned grounded candidate 5 (street kiosk); browser verdict selects A from requested prototypes instead, as the user's instruction to select the visually strongest outranks the random assignment. Retained lesson from saturated module challenger: color must own a full region, not become colored buttons on the old layout. Other speculative worlds are declined because they weaken bakery identification and/or shopping clarity; no foreign motif is borrowed.
